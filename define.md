# MissionX — landing-page definition

MissionX is an independent educational browser-based space mission design project by Team NASA WARRIORS, created for the NASA International Space Apps Challenge 2026. Not affiliated with or endorsed by NASA.

Primary audience: students 12–18; secondary: educators and space enthusiasts. Teach the relationship between mission goals, instruments, mass, power, data, budget, launch and science return. Mission experiences: Moon Mapping, Sun Watch (four satellites), Mars Orbiter. Intended game loop: GOAL → BUILD → VERIFY → LAUNCH → RESULT.

## Content hierarchy
1. Hero: emotional hook, orbital visual, start briefing.
2. How it works: interactive five-stage editorial presentation.
3. Missions: three cinematic destination briefs, difficulty and objectives.
4. Engineering: interactive instrument toggle and illustrative resource meters.
5. Mission control: launch, space, ground, user; simplified educational model.
6. Final invitation: begin mission briefing.
7. Footer: project context, links, team and non-affiliation disclaimer.

## Complete product phase — current scope, 2026-11-14
The previous landing-only boundary is historical. Implement the full six-screen journey: Landing → Goal → Build → Verify → Launch → Result → Debrief → Redesign. Three missions have distinct science objectives and four numeric requirements: mass, full mission budget (including launch), science capability, and design lifetime. Sun Watch models four identical coordinated spacecraft. Parts are selected by subsystem; science instruments may be combined. A compatible spacecraft needs a structure, solar supply, battery, communications, computer and attitude control. Instrument compatibility, energy through eclipse, downlink and mission orbit are checked. All checks must pass before launch. Seeded events can degrade lifetime, power and data, or cause launch failure; results are recomputed from these effects, not cosmetic animation. Saved runs include immutable configuration, seed and report for meaningful comparison. History is capped locally. No accounts/database. Mentor runs without keys via deterministic teaching advice; optional remote model is server-only. English/Urdu and team mode remain optional, not core requirements.
